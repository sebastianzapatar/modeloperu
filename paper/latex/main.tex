\begin{abstract}
Peru combines abundant renewable resources with a generation mix still built on
hydropower and natural gas, a longitudinal grid linking three load zones in series,
and generation and transmission projects that advance on very different timescales.
Which of these uncertainties governs the system's trajectory determines where planning
effort should go. We build a system dynamics model of the Peruvian power system,
coupled to a congestion-aware dispatch layer, and simulate it monthly from 2025 to 2050
over a designed experiment of 129 runs: a central case, eight one-at-a-time sensitivity
runs, and 120 Latin-hypercube Monte Carlo samples of four uncertain drivers --- fuel
prices, demand growth, and project lead times on the generation and the transmission
side. Demand growth dominates every outcome, with
Spearman rank correlations of 0.99 for transmission length, 0.97 for installed
capacity and $-0.75$ for the average price, while the other three drivers stay below
0.27 in absolute value. A $\pm$20\,\% band on the 2050 demand level moves required
transmission by $-18.9$\,\% to $+23.8$\,\% and installed capacity by $-24.0$\,\% to
$+29.3$\,\%. The price moves against intuition: faster demand growth lowers the
long-run average price, pulling forward low-cost renewable capacity the central case
defers. The least-cost trajectory is consequently the most transmission-intensive,
requiring 26\,\% more network. For Peru the binding planning question is therefore not
how to hedge fuel-price risk but whether the grid can be delivered fast enough to serve
demand growth that is itself the dominant unknown.
\end{abstract}
